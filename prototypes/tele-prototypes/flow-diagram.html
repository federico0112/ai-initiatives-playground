Wholesale invoice dispute flow

How one case moves from an invoice that is 8% above expectation to an analyst-approved decision. Every conclusion links back to a source record or a deterministic calculation, and the flow pauses to ask for evidence instead of guessing an amount.

rules deterministic code, same input gives same output agent model reasoning, bounded by cited evidence analyst human decision outside the customer’s systems, or a paused state

Which claims can support a dispute amount

Step 4 sorts every statement into one of four classes. Step 5 only counts the first two toward the supported amount. The other two become the unresolved amount, which is what drives a request for evidence.

counts toward supported

Verified fact

Read directly from a record. The invoice bills destination X at rate R from date D.

counts toward supported

Calculation

A deterministic result over cited rows. Re-rating these minutes at the contracted rate gives amount A.

unresolved only

Interpretation

A likely explanation the records don’t prove. The carrier probably applied the new rate before its notice period ended.

unresolved only

Missing evidence

A record the case needs but doesn’t have. No rate notification on file for this destination change.

How step 6 picks a decision

DecisionWhen
Approve invoiceSupported dispute amount is zero and the unresolved amount is below the materiality threshold.
Partial disputeSupported amount is above zero but covers only part of the variance, and the remainder is explained or immaterial.
Full disputeSupported amount covers the whole variance.
Request evidenceUnresolved amount is material, or a blocking gap remains, and the contract’s dispute window still allows time. Close to the deadline, the agent instead recommends disputing the supported amount and reserving the rest.

What changed from the original seven steps

  1. Step 2 can stop the case. A blocking data issue pauses it instead of passing bad data into reconciliation.
  2. Requesting evidence is a paused state, not an end point. New records go back through validation before anything downstream reruns.
  3. Step 1 records the dispute deadline from the contract, so step 6 can weigh waiting for evidence against the window closing.
  4. The evidence ledger is shared by all steps from the start. The audit record is compiled from it rather than written at the end.
  5. The analyst can return a case with notes, and step 6 revises the recommendation.
  6. Each step is marked as rules, agent or analyst. Amounts are only ever produced by rules steps.

Open questions for the next pass

  • Decided: thresholds, adjustable later: open a case when the variance is above 2% of the invoiced total, and request evidence when the unresolved amount is above 1%. Both also respect the contract’s minimum dispute amount when one exists. The thresholds are settings, and each case records the values it used, so changing them later never rewrites past decisions.
  • Should “full dispute” stay as its own outcome, or is it a partial dispute that happens to cover everything?
